# Existing solutions for the Citi rural learning-to-livelihood challenge

Research date: 17 October 2026

## Research conclusion

Many important parts of this challenge already operate in real products and programs. The strongest references for product design are iDreamCareer, Quest Alliance's MyQuest, Antarang, YouthHub, Haqdarshak, and Pratham's skilling programs. Kolibri and Sunbird provide reusable open-source foundations. FarmerChat provides a particularly relevant example of multilingual AI used by rural communities and frontline workers.

The recommended opportunity is a teacher-assisted service that helps a student explore careers through small practical activities, compare feasible pathways, complete learning tasks, and act on verified opportunities. This is a proposed combination of existing patterns, not a claim of worldwide novelty or a previously validated intervention.

This is documentary research using product documentation, official program publications, research papers, and evaluation summaries. It does not constitute hands-on testing of the applications, an audit of reported numbers, or confirmation that their APIs are available to this team.

## How to read the evidence

- Randomized evaluation: strong evidence for the particular intervention, population, and outcome studied. It does not establish effectiveness for every deployment or a different product using a similar feature.
- Observational evaluation or surveys: useful evidence of experience or associations, with possible selection and reporting biases.
- Operating program and reported outcomes: evidence of implementation and monitored results, without necessarily identifying what caused those results.
- Product availability and usage: evidence that something has been deployed, not proof that it improves earnings or learning.

Reach, registrations, course certificates, applications, placements, and sustained employment are different measures. The report keeps them separate. Historical figures retain their dates.

## Closest precedents

### 1. iDreamCareer / PathShila

**What exists:** Career information, assessments, counselling, education pathways, exams, scholarships, and institutional dashboards. UNICEF's August 2021 account documents the CBSE partnership, multilingual career information, and training of two teachers/counsellors per school. This is a strong precedent for combining a career knowledge base with school-based support.

**Evidence:** Documented deployment through education-system partnerships. The 2021 catalogue sizes are historical, not current verified counts. The current PathShila page describes continuing government/CSR programs and a third-party quasi-experimental study, but its brief metric description is insufficient to treat it as definitive causal evidence.

**Adaptation:** Connect each career to education prerequisites, funding, and teacher follow-up.

Sources: [UNICEF deployment account, 2021](https://www.unicef.org/india/stories/experience-personalized-unique-career-journey), [current PathShila program](https://idreamcareer.com/social-impact/).

### 2. Quest Alliance / MyQuest

**What exists:** Employability learning, career exploration, educator support, and vocational-institution partnerships. Its 2024–25 report describes a MyQuest pilot with 30,000 users, over 300 short lessons, 1,100 job cards, 160+ trades, and five languages. Earlier documentation describes offline content distribution and facilitator tools.

**Evidence:** The same report says 65% of 128,046 graduating ITI learners transitioned into jobs or further education. That is a combined program outcome; it is neither a jobs-only placement rate nor an estimated effect of the app. The report describes GenAI learning as a next milestone.

**Adaptation:** Short career-linked lessons, trade cards, and facilitator interventions.

Sources: [Quest annual report 2024–25, especially page 3](https://www.questalliance.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Annual-report-2024-25.pdf), [historical offline/facilitator architecture](https://www.questalliance.net/news/the-hub).

### 3. Antarang Foundation

**What exists:** A four-year career education model for grades 9–12, involving classroom facilitators, individual counselling, parents, alumni support, an LMS for facilitators, and a public WhatsApp career chatbot.

**Evidence:** An operating school-partnership program with published annual reports. The reviewed materials establish delivery and program structure; they do not establish a randomized effect of the chatbot. Some website counters render as zero, so they are excluded here.

**Adaptation:** Match activities to the student's school stage. Include parents and facilitators in decisions, and preserve support beyond a single assessment.

Sources: [program model](https://antarangfoundation.org/what-we-do/), [access and program FAQs](https://antarangfoundation.org/faqs/), [reports](https://antarangfoundation.org/reports-and-financials/).

### 4. UNICEF YuWaah / YouthHub

**What exists:** An aggregator connecting young people to jobs, skilling, and volunteering. UNICEF describes AI matching based on interests and skills, blue/grey-collar opportunities, and availability in nine languages.

**Evidence:** A deployed platform described by UNICEF, with an Android listing. The reviewed product page does not establish how much YouthHub alone improves employment. YuWaah-wide reach figures should not be presented as YouthHub's active users or placements.

**Adaptation:** Reuse a common student profile across learning and opportunities, and include volunteering as a way to gain initial experience.

Sources: [UNICEF product description](https://www.unicef.org/india/economic-opportunities-young-people), [Android listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.yuwaah.android).

### 5. UNICEF Passport to Earning (P2E)

**What exists:** Free job-relevant digital learning and certification, including financial literacy and digital productivity.

**Evidence:** An October 2023 Ministry of Education release reports more than one million young people skilled and certified in India. These are certifications, not a million verified jobs. UNICEF's platform terms describe online, app-based, and offline learning platforms, but this does not establish that every Indian P2E course is available offline.

**Adaptation:** Give students a small, relevant first course and a visible record of completion before asking them to navigate a large catalogue.

Sources: [India certification milestone, 2023](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=1967153&lang=2&reg=48), [UNICEF platform terms](https://www.unicef.org/learningpassport/learning-passport-terms-of-use).

### 6. Haqdarshak

**What exists:** Eligibility screening and assistance accessing government benefits through technology and trained community agents. Support extends beyond discovery to applications and follow-up.

**Evidence:** Acumen's 2024 investor case reports 6.3 million citizens reached and $2.1 billion in benefits unlocked. It also describes survey findings and agents' livelihoods. This is investor/program evidence, not a randomized evaluation. Its dated citizen counts should not be combined with differently defined family counts on the current company site.

**Adaptation:** Turn each opportunity into an actionable case: eligibility, missing information, documents, application route, responsible helper, and status.

Sources: [Acumen case study](https://acumen.org/case-studies/haqdarshak/), [current product capabilities](https://www.haqdarshak.com/).

### 7. Buddy4Study

**What exists:** Scholarship discovery, application support, and scholarship-program management for fund providers. Published guidance covers application verification and status tracking.

**Evidence:** HDFC Bank's CSR reporting identifies Buddy4Study as an implementation partner for its ECSS scholarship program. This supports real scholarship operations. It does not validate the matching accuracy of every listing or imply that applying guarantees funding.

**Adaptation:** A reusable student application profile, deadline reminders, document readiness, and an understandable application-status timeline.

Sources: [platform model](https://www.buddy4study.com/about-us), [application guidance](https://www.buddy4study.com/article/mastering-scholarship-applications-on-buddy4study), [HDFC CSR report FY2024–25](https://v.hdfc.bank.in/content/dam/hdfc-aem-microsites/csr/pdfs/hdfc-bank-parivartan-csr-report-fy-24-25.pdf).

### 8. Pratham Youth & Skilling

**What exists:** Counselling, trade exploration, theory, practical training, employer connections, and post-placement support for disadvantaged youth. Its model progresses from short awareness courses to deeper training.

**Evidence:** A July 2025 program account reports over 28,500 youth trained and over 23,000 placed during the preceding year. These are provider-reported program outcomes. Current program documentation describes tracking and mentoring youth for one year after graduation. Outcomes come from a blended training and employment program, not software alone.

**Adaptation:** Let students try a vocation before committing, account for travel constraints, and follow up after a transition.

Sources: [community training camps and outcomes, 2025](https://pratham.org.uk/community-camps-bringing-vocational-training-closer-to-home/), [training model](https://www.pratham.org/programs/education/vocational-training/).

### 9. Mentor Together / Mentor To Go

**What exists:** Mentor screening and training, preference-based matching, structured work-readiness activities, and monitored mentoring relationships.

**Evidence:** The organization reports over 12,700 structured mentorships. Its much larger mentee figure includes sign-ups and should not be described as completed mentorships. Program documentation describes before-and-after measurement using standardized surveys; that is weaker than a randomized comparison.

**Adaptation:** Build mentor onboarding, availability, guided activities, and facilitator oversight. A directory of mentor profiles alone would omit much of the operating model.

Sources: [reported scale](https://mentortogether.org/), [matching, delivery, and measurement](https://mentortogether.org/mentoring-program/).

## Strong references for individual capabilities

### 10. Mindspark

**What exists:** Adaptive instruction that diagnoses a learner's level and adjusts practice.

**Evidence:** A randomized evaluation with 619 mostly grade 6–9 students in Delhi during 2015–16 found that offering free access to the program increased independently measured math scores by 0.37 standard deviations and Hindi scores by 0.23 over about 4.5 months. The intervention combined software with instructor-led small-group teaching. It did not test career matching or employment outcomes.

**Adaptation:** Diagnose prerequisite skills and update the next learning task from observed performance. Do not infer that a generated roadmap produces the same effect.

Source: [J-PAL evaluation](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/disrupting-education-evidence-technology-aided-instruction-india).

### 11. Kolibri / Learning Equality

**What exists:** An open-source platform for offline content, lessons, quizzes, educator dashboards, local classroom servers, and Android homework synchronized back to school.

**Evidence:** Product documentation and implementation/evaluation reports show use in low-resource settings. Learning Equality's evidence page also includes studies of its predecessor KA Lite; those findings must not all be attributed to current Kolibri.

**Adaptation:** Download a small learning package, complete it offline, and synchronize progress later. A teacher's local server can distribute resources without every student using mobile data.

Sources: [product and synchronization model](https://learningequality.org/kolibri/about-kolibri/), [implementation evidence](https://learningequality.org/impact/our-impact/), [MIT-licensed repository](https://github.com/learningequality/kolibri).

### 12. Digital Green / FarmerChat

**What exists:** Multilingual agricultural advice using voice, text, images, local context, retrieved knowledge, and frontline-worker support. Agriculture is an adjacent sector, but the access challenges closely resemble this brief.

**Evidence:** A 2024 developer-authored paper documents deployment to over 15,000 farmers and over 300,000 questions across four countries. FY2025 reporting includes survey evidence of advice adoption and worker time savings, while saying full-scale impact evaluations remain underway. It does not prove a causal income increase.

**Adaptation:** Brief voice exchanges, progressive onboarding, contextual answers, and human escalation.

Sources: [deployment and architecture paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.08916v2), [FY2025 evidence report](https://digitalgreen.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Digital-Green-FY25-Annual-Report.pdf).

### 13. Google Read Along / Bolo

**What exists:** Voice-supported reading practice and feedback. Google's 2020 documentation describes offline operation after downloading content and speech analysis on the device.

**Evidence:** Google reported an independent-partner assessment of over 3,500 students in five Indian states: 40% more app users improved at least one reading level than non-users. The public summary alone does not establish randomized assignment. This is historical literacy evidence for younger children, not evidence for career guidance.

**Adaptation:** Audio help, a simple interaction loop, multiple learner profiles, and downloaded activities. Offline recognition in a constrained reading task does not establish offline general conversation.

Sources: [offline product design, 2020](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/early-access-read-along/), [assessment summary, 2020](https://blog.google/intl/en-in/products/google-companies/reinforcing-our-commitment-to/).

### 14. Forage

**What exists:** Free, self-paced job simulations designed with employers. Its site lists over 300 simulations and over 100 employers, and explicitly says employers pay to host programs.

**Evidence:** An operating product and commercial distribution model. Enrollment counts and promotional employability comparisons should not be treated as randomized evidence of job creation. Its predominantly corporate career context needs adaptation for rural vocations.

**Adaptation:** Offer a small sample of real work before recommending a long course: a customer conversation, design task, bookkeeping exercise, or safe diagnostic reasoning activity. Create original local tasks rather than copying simulations.

Source: [Forage product and business model](https://www.theforage.com/about).

Neither open source nor multilingual support guarantees performance on inexpensive Android phones. For a first prototype, host heavy inference on a server and preserve meaningful offline functions locally. Benchmark before promising on-device conversational AI.

## What the research suggests building

The following are design proposals derived from the precedents, not already measured outcomes.

### Option A: Teacher-assisted career action plan — recommended first

Help each student move from an initial voice conversation to an informed choice and a completed next action.

1. Ask short questions about interests, school level, language, available time, costs, location, and mobility. Let the student or teacher correct the resulting profile.
2. Show a manageable comparison of pathways, including aspirational options and vocational/non-STEM routes. Explain constraints and how to overcome them without treating family income as a permanent limit.
3. Offer a short practical career activity so the student can explore the work itself. Present the result as evidence to discuss, not a definitive aptitude label.
4. Identify a prerequisite skill gap and assign the next relevant learning task.
5. Attach real, source-linked opportunities and explain eligibility, missing information, documents, and application steps.
6. Give the teacher an intervention queue showing who needs help, why, and which deadline matters.
7. Track action completion and verified outcomes over time.

Inspiration: iDreamCareer and Antarang for guidance, Forage and Pratham for exploration, Mindspark for diagnostic learning, Haqdarshak for follow-through, and Kolibri for offline delivery.

The proposed differentiation is continuity across these steps and practical facilitator support. Interviews and a competitor walkthrough would still be needed to establish whether this addresses an underserved local need.

### Option B: Opportunity-readiness passport

For older students, maintain a compact record of completed activities, teacher-reviewed work, learning evidence, documents ready, and application history. Use it to suggest the next requirement for a particular apprenticeship, scholarship, or mentor session.

Combine experience-building ideas from YouthHub and Forage with Buddy4Study-style application management and Mentor To Go-style structured mentoring. Evidence should say who verified it and when. Do not call an internal badge an accredited qualification or imply that employers already accept it.

This option needs genuine partner opportunities to demonstrate value. Begin with partner-submitted listings or official source links, and keep a visible distinction between recommendations, submitted applications, and confirmed acceptance.

### Option C: Facilitator-run local learning groups

Let a teacher or qualified local trainer assemble short modules around a local opportunity, assign them to a group, and track completion. A possible later revenue feature is paid modules or institution-sponsored cohorts.

Use Quest's educator support and Kolibri's group learning as precedents. Preserve a review process for training quality and verify claims about job access. A marketplace adds work in trainer recruitment, payments, quality control, and learner support; it is a later expansion unless the team already has a trainer network.

## A focused prototype scope

Suggested scope, to be adjusted to the hackathon's actual time limit:

- One district or comparable local context, while documenting how new districts would be added.
- Two well-tested regional languages, rather than claiming broad coverage from translation alone.
- A curated starter set of career pathways including non-STEM and vocational options.
- A few original career exploration activities with transparent evaluation rubrics.
- Real opportunity records with eligibility rules, source URLs, and last-verified dates.
- A student flow and a teacher flow using the same underlying records.
- A genuine offline learning task and reliable synchronization demonstration.

The demonstration should include changing a profile fact and showing the resulting eligibility/pathway change. This is more informative than presenting several static screens.

### Offline behavior to implement explicitly

| Available without internet | Requires connection or fresh verification |
|---|---|
| Saved profile and pathway | Generating a new server-based AI response |
| Downloaded learning activities and cached audio | Fetching new courses or fresh listings |
| Local progress and draft application checklist | Checking a current deadline or available seat |
| Teacher notes queued for synchronization | Sending an application to an external platform |

Show when cached data was checked. Distinguish a locally saved draft from a successfully submitted application. If voice is recorded while offline for later processing, explain that behavior in the interface.

### Keep eligibility separate from generated explanations

Store criteria in structured records and evaluate them consistently. Let the model explain the result using the cited scheme record. Missing information should produce an unresolved result rather than an invented answer. Final eligibility remains subject to the provider's current rules and verification.

Every recommendation should be traceable to its source, date, and relevant profile facts. Retrieval alone does not guarantee that the model interpreted those facts correctly.

## Monetization lessons and hypotheses

There are credible precedents for someone other than the student funding the service:

- Forage states that employers pay to host programs while students use simulations free.
- Haqdarshak describes organization-funded delivery, with assisted service fees also appearing in its operating model.
- PathShila works through government, CSR, and nonprofit partnerships.

For this team, the first revenue hypothesis should be an institution/NGO/CSR customer paying for facilitator tools, implementation support, and measurable learner progress. Later hypotheses include employer-sponsored career activities and optional paid trainer modules. These require buyer interviews and willingness-to-pay testing. Existing partner-funded models do not establish that a particular school or NGO will buy this prototype.

Measure the main costs: inference and speech processing, content maintenance, facilitator time, onboarding, support, and opportunity verification. Revenue projections should identify who pays, for what service, and how delivery costs change with usage.

## Validation before claiming it works

A suggested initial pilot is 20–30 students and two or three facilitators. This is a proposed usability pilot, not a sample sufficient to prove long-term impact.

Check whether students can complete the voice/profile flow, understand the pathway comparison, find the next action, finish a downloaded task, and explain why an opportunity may fit. Observe actual Android devices and network conditions. Include shared-device usage and switching between students.

Measure:

- Accuracy of eligibility results against manually reviewed cases, including missing information.
- Whether citations actually support recommendations and whether deadlines are current.
- Voice task completion and correction rates in each tested language.
- Successful offline progress recovery and synchronization without duplication or loss.
- Student understanding and completed actions, alongside drop-off points.
- Facilitator time per student and time required to resolve a blocked case.
- Over longer follow-up: verified applications, acceptances, course completion, retained employment, and actual earnings where applicable.

Use the results to narrow the product. Do not equate chatbot engagement with career clarity, a certificate with a job, or a short pilot with demonstrated livelihood impact.

## Recommended study order

1. MyQuest and iDreamCareer: understand how much of the proposed platform already exists.
2. Haqdarshak and Buddy4Study: study the transition from information to completed applications.
3. Antarang and Mentor To Go: study the human support system around the technology.
4. Pratham and Forage: design original, practical career exploration activities.
5. Kolibri, DIKSHA, and FarmerChat: choose offline and voice patterns that fit the actual prototype.
6. Mindspark: understand the evidence for diagnosing learning needs and adapting instruction.

The defensible claim at this stage is that these patterns have operating precedents and, in some cases, measured results. Their combination in this team's product remains a hypothesis to build and test.
